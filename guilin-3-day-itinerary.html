High-Speed Rail (高铁 - Gaotie)

China's High-Speed Rail is a modern marvel. It's often faster, cheaper, and more convenient than flying. Trains run at 300-350 km/h (186-217 mph) and are impeccably punctual.

How to Book Tickets

Foreigners have two reliable ways to book train tickets:

  • Trip.com App: The easiest and most reliable method in English. It charges a tiny convenience fee but saves you headaches.
  • 12306 Official App: Now available in English. No fees, but verification can occasionally be glitchy for foreign passports.

Boarding with a Passport

There are no physical tickets!

Your passport is your ticket. When you arrive at the train station security and the boarding gate, you simply hand your physical passport to the staff member in the manual lane (usually the far left or far right aisle), or place it on the passport scanner.

Crucial Tip: Arrive Early

Chinese train stations are built like massive airport terminals. Arrive at least 45-60 minutes before your departure time. You will need to go through security checks and ID verification to enter the building.

Ride-Hailing with DiDi (滴滴)

Uber does not exist in China. The dominant ride-hailing app is DiDi. It is incredibly cheap, safe, and efficient.

  • No Standalone App Needed: Open your Alipay app, search for "DiDi", and use the Mini Program. The interface will automatically translate to English.
  • Auto-Translation: The in-app chat translates your English messages into Chinese for the driver, and vice-versa.
  • Payment: Rides are automatically deducted from your linked Visa/Mastercard via Alipay when the ride finishes.

Metros and Subways

Every major Chinese city has an extensive, modern, and cheap subway system. Signs and ticket machines always have an English option.

  • Alipay Transport Code: The easiest way to ride. In Alipay, tap "Transport", select the city you are in, and generate a QR code to scan at the turnstile.
  • Apple Pay (Transit): You can add a Shanghai or Beijing transit card directly to your Apple Wallet. It works even if your phone battery dies (Express Transit mode).

Book Your High-Speed Train

Secure your seats in advance, especially during summer and holidays. English interface, foreign credit cards accepted.

China High-Speed Rail: The World's Largest Network

China's high-speed rail (HSR) network spans over 40,000 km — more than the rest of the world combined. Trains hit 350 km/h (217 mph), turning what was once a 12-hour journey (Beijing to Shanghai) into just 4.5 hours. Here's what every tourist needs to know.

🚄 350 km/h

Top speed of China's fastest trains (Fuxing and Hexie models). Beijing to Shanghai in 4.5 hours, Chengdu to Xi'an in 3.5 hours.

🇨🇳 100+ Cities

Connected by high-speed rail, including all major tourist destinations: Beijing, Shanghai, Xi'an, Chengdu, Guilin, Kunming, Hangzhou.

⏱️ 99% On-Time

Chinese HSR has one of the world's best punctuality records. Delays over 5 minutes are rare on major routes.

How to Buy Train Tickets as a Foreigner

Option 1: Trip.com (Easiest for Tourists)

Trip.com is the best option for foreigners. It has a full English interface, accepts international credit cards, and charges a small booking fee (~¥20-30 per ticket). You can book up to 15 days in advance.

Option 2: 12306.cn (Official, Cheapest)

The official China Railway app (12306) now has an English version and accepts foreign passports. No booking fee, but the interface can be confusing. You'll need to verify your passport at a station counter on first use.

Option 3: Buy at the Station

You can buy tickets at any train station. Bring your passport — it's required. Go to the "English Service" window at major stations (Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou). Expect queues during holidays.

City Metro Systems: Getting Around

Beijing Metro

27 lines, 500+ stations. Fares from ¥3. Signs and announcements in English. Use the Yitongxing (亿通行) app for QR code payment — works with foreign cards via Alipay. Avoid Line 1 and Line 10 during rush hour (7:30-9:00 AM, 5:30-7:00 PM).

Shanghai Metro

20 lines, 500+ stations. The world's longest metro system. Use the Daduhui (大都会) app. All signs are bilingual. The Maglev from Pudong Airport to Longyang Road hits 431 km/h — a tourist attraction in itself (¥50, 8 minutes).

Shenzhen Metro

16 lines. One of the cleanest metros globally. English signs everywhere. Tap-and-go with Alipay transport code.

Ride-Hailing: Didi for Foreigners

Didi Chuxing is China's Uber. The app now has an English interface and accepts international credit cards via the DiDi English version. Key tips:

  • Download before arriving — The app may not be available in some app stores once you're in China
  • Set pickup carefully — Chinese addresses are complex. Use landmarks (hotel name, mall entrance)
  • Have your destination in Chinese — Show the driver the Chinese characters, even if you use the English app
  • Fare estimate: ¥25-50 for a 20-minute ride in most cities
  • Alternative: Alipay's built-in "Transport" mini-program also calls DiDi rides without a separate app

FAQ: China Transport

Can I rent a car in China as a foreigner?

Technically yes, but practically no. China doesn't recognize foreign driver's licenses without a Chinese translation test. Even with a license, driving in Chinese cities is chaotic and parking is a nightmare. Stick to trains, metro, and Didi.

Do I need to book train tickets in advance?

Yes, especially during Chinese holidays (Spring Festival, October Golden Week). Tickets sell out weeks in advance. Book 1-2 weeks ahead for normal travel, 3-4 weeks for holidays.

Is the Beijing-Shanghai bullet train worth it vs flying?

For most tourists, yes. Door-to-door, the train (4.5h) is competitive with flying (2h flight + 2h airport transfers/security). Trains are more comfortable, have better scenery, and are more reliable. Take the train.
