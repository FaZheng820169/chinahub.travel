🇨🇳 144-Hour Visa-Free Transit (2026 Policy)

China's 144-hour (6-day) visa-free transit policy is one of the easiest ways for many nationalities to visit China without the hassle of applying for a visa in advance.

What is the 144-Hour Visa-Free Transit Policy?

The 144-hour visa-free transit policy allows passport holders from 53 eligible countries to enter China without a visa, stay for up to 144 hours (6 days), and move freely within the designated administrative regions — as long as they are in transit to a third country or region.

This policy was significantly expanded in December 2024, now covering 37 entry/exit ports across 24 provinces, autonomous regions, and municipalities. This means you can now visit major destinations like Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou, Shenzhen, Chengdu, Hangzhou, Xi'an, and more — all without a visa.

📌 Key Requirement

You MUST be traveling from one country to another (or region, including Hong Kong, Macau, or Taiwan). A round-trip from your home country to China and back does NOT qualify.

🛫 Step-by-Step: How to Use the 144-Hour Visa-Free Policy

1

Book an eligible itinerary

Your trip must originate in one country and have a final destination in a different country or region (Hong Kong, Macau, or Taiwan count as "different"). Example: New York → Beijing (6 days) → Tokyo. Or: London → Shanghai (3 days) → Beijing (3 days) → Seoul.

2

Notify your airline

When checking in for your flight to China, show your onward ticket to the airline staff. They need to verify you meet the transit requirement before boarding you.

3

Arrive at a designated port

Enter China through one of the 37 designated ports (including Beijing Capital, Beijing Daxing, Shanghai Pudong, Shanghai Hongqiao, Guangzhou Baiyun, Shenzhen Bao'an, Chengdu Tianfu, Xi'an Xianyang, etc.).

4

Fill out the Temporary Entry Card

On the flight, you'll receive a Temporary Entry Card for Foreigners (外国人临时入境卡). Fill it out before arriving at immigration. If you don't receive one, ask a flight attendant or pick one up at the port.

5

Go to the "144-Hour Visa-Free" counter

At immigration, look for signs saying "144-Hour Visa-Free Transit" or "Temporary Entry". Present your passport (valid for at least 3 months), onward ticket, and the completed entry card. The officer will stamp your passport with a stay-until date.

6

Enjoy your stay — and exit before the deadline!

You can travel freely within the approved region (e.g., if you enter via Shanghai, you can visit all of Jiangsu, Zhejiang, and Shanghai). You MUST exit China before the date stamped in your passport. Overstaying carries heavy penalties.

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • ❌ Round-trip ticket only: A ticket from the US to China and back to the US does NOT qualify. You need a third-country destination.
  • ❌ Expired passport: Your passport must be valid for at least 3 months from entry.
  • ❌ Overstaying: The 144 hours start at 00:00 the day after arrival. Leaving even 1 day late can result in a fine, detention, or a future entry ban.
  • ❌ Wrong exit port: You must exit through a designated port in the same region where you entered.

📋 Chinese Tourist Visa (L Visa) — Full Guide

If you're not eligible for visa-free transit, or you want to visit China as your sole destination, you'll need to apply for a Tourist (L) Visa at a Chinese embassy or consulate in your home country.

L Visa Types & Duration

Single Entry

Valid for 3 months, allows one entry. Max stay: usually 30 or 60 days. Best for: one-time trips.

Double Entry

Valid for 6 months, allows two entries. Max stay per entry: 30–60 days. Best for: visiting Hong Kong or Taiwan mid-trip and re-entering China.

Multiple Entry

Valid for 6 or 12 months, unlimited entries. Max stay per entry: 30–90 days. Best for: frequent travelers and business visitors.

📄 Required Documents (L Visa Application)

✅

Valid Passport

Must have at least 6 months validity remaining and at least 2 blank visa pages.

✅

Visa Application Form (Form V.2013)

Complete online at cova.mfa.gov.cn. Print and sign it.

✅

Recent Passport Photo

One 48mm × 33mm color photo with white background, taken within the last 6 months. Many consulates require an online upload before your appointment.

✅

Proof of Travel (Round-Trip Flight + Hotel Booking)

Some consulates require confirmed round-trip flight tickets and hotel reservations covering your entire stay. Check your local consulate's specific requirements — some accept tentative bookings.

✅

Additional Documents (if applicable)

Former Chinese citizens need their Chinese passport cancellation certificate. US/Canadian citizens of Chinese origin may need additional proof of status.

📑 Other Common Chinese Visa Types

M Visa (Business)

For commercial and trade activities. Requires an invitation letter from a Chinese business partner. Valid for 3 months to 10 years depending on nationality.

Z Visa (Work)

Required for legal employment in China. Your employer in China must first obtain a Work Permit Notice before you can apply for the Z visa.

X Visa (Student)

For studying in China. X1 is for programs longer than 180 days; X2 is for short-term study (less than 180 days).

Q Visa (Family Visit)

For visiting Chinese citizens or foreigners with permanent residence in China. Q1 is for long-term stays; Q2 is for short-term visits.

❓ Visa & Entry/Exit FAQ

Can I extend my stay in China?

Extensions are possible but not guaranteed. You must apply at the local Public Security Bureau (PSB) Exit-Entry Administration office before your current visa expires. Tourist visa extensions are typically granted for 30 additional days. Overstaying and then applying is a serious violation.

Do I need a visa to visit Hong Kong or Macau?

Hong Kong and Macau have separate visa policies from mainland China. Many nationalities can enter Hong Kong visa-free for 7–180 days. If you leave mainland China to visit Hong Kong, you'll need a new Chinese visa (or be using a multiple-entry visa) to re-enter the mainland.

What happens if I overstay my visa?

Overstaying is a serious offense in China. Penalties include: daily fines (typically ¥500/day), detention, deportation, and a potential ban from re-entering China for 1–10 years. If you realize you've overstayed, go to the local PSB immediately and explain your situation — they may be more lenient if you self-report.

Can I work or study on a tourist (L) visa?

No. Engaging in employment or enrolling in a degree program while on a tourist visa is illegal and can result in deportation and a multi-year entry ban. You must obtain the appropriate Z visa (work) or X visa (study) before engaging in these activities.

💡 Pro Tips for Visa Applications

📌 Apply Early

Apply at least 1 month before travel. Embassies get busy before holidays.

📌 Check Passport Validity

Ensure at least 6 months validity and 2 blank visa pages.

📌 Keep Copies

Carry a photocopy of your visa and passport data page when sightseeing.

📌 Register Within 24h

If staying in a private residence, you must register with local police within 24 hours.
